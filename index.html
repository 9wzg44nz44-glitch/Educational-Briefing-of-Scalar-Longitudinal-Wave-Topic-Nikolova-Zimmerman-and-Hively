SLIDE 01

Introduction to Scalar Longitudinal Waves (SLW)

Communications Data Links
Beyond Classical Transverse EM

Scalar Longitudinal Waves (SLW) represent an area of advanced and experimental electromagnetism exploring non-standard propagation modes involving scalar potentials, longitudinal electric fields, and/or vector potential (A-field) waves. These may enable unique communication properties such as altered attenuation, penetration characteristics, or information transfer via potentials (inspired by the Aharonov-Bohm effect).

Historical Context

Extensions of Maxwell's equations, Tesla's longitudinal wave concepts, and modern reinterpretations of potentials as physically meaningful quantities.

Experimental Claims

Reports of km-range links at UHF using specialized antennas/detectors (plasma tubes, irrotational current structures) with potentially different scaling than 1/r² power loss.

Two Key Approaches

Nikolova-Zimmerman (vector potential / momentum waves) vs. Hively (Extended Electrodynamics + SLW via irrotational fields).

QEX 2011 • McMaster Reports • US Patents 8,165,531 & 9,306,527
SLIDE 02

Key Differences at a Glance

COMPARATIVE OVERVIEW
NIKOLOVA–ZIMMERMAN

Vector Potential / Momentum Waves

  • Core Focus: Physical significance and propagation of the magnetic vector potential A and longitudinally-polarized momentum waves.
  • Key Experiments: 1.296 GHz links over ~1.5 km claimed; waveguide-contained Tx to suppress TEM; plasma/fluorescent tube detectors.
  • Theoretical Basis: Detection of time-dependent electromagnetic potentials; possible independence of potentials from E/B in certain configurations.
  • Patents/Papers: US 8,165,531 B2 (Nikolova & Zimmerman); QEX Jul/Aug 2011 (Zimmerman); McMaster CEM-R-46 Report (2007).
HIVELY (EED)

Scalar-Longitudinal Waves via Extended Electrodynamics

  • Core Focus: Scalar potential + longitudinal electric field (SLW); irrotational (gradient-driven) currents and fields in Extended Electrodynamics (EED).
  • Key Concepts: Gauge-free dynamical scalar field; novel SLW antennas exploiting irrotational J; predictions of propagating scalar field without E/B in some modes.
  • Theoretical Basis: EED extends classical ED with additional terms in energy/momentum balance; irrotational E and current components enable SLW generation/detection.
  • Patents/Papers: US 9,306,527 B1 (Hively 2016); Physics Essays 32(1) 2019 (Hively & Loebl); Reed papers on EED validation criteria.
Both approaches seek to explain and exploit phenomena beyond classical Hertzian transverse EM waves, but differ in emphasis (vector potential vs. longitudinal E + scalar) and hardware implications.
SLIDE 03

Theoretical Foundations & Differing Physics

NIKOLOVA–ZIMMERMAN
Vector Potential Waves

Emphasizes the magnetic vector potential A as a physically propagating entity capable of carrying momentum and information. Longitudinally-polarized momentum waves may exist independently or alongside classical fields in specific setups (e.g., inside waveguides that cutoff TEM modes).

Detection often via plasma tubes responding to time-varying potentials (possibly via induced E or direct A-effects, cf. Aharonov-Bohm).
Key Experimental Signature

Signal differences with bias reversal on U-shaped plasma tubes; communication demonstrated at 1296 MHz over significant distances where conventional explanations were tested and alternative interpretations proposed.

HIVELY / EED
Extended Electrodynamics (EED)

EED augments classical Maxwell equations with irrotational (curl-free, gradient-driven) components of E, J, and potentials. Introduces a dynamical scalar field. Predicts SLW composed of scalar potential + longitudinal E that can propagate with distinct characteristics.

Gradient-driven (irrotational) current → novel SLW antenna geometries (per 2016 patent). Additional terms in energy & momentum balance equations.
Key Predicted/Observed Features

1/r attenuation in some media/tests; isotropic radiation from monopole-like structures; power levels comparable to TEM; no skin-effect constraint in certain conductive media; irrotational current as source.

SLIDE 04

Generation & Transmission

NIKOLOVA–ZIMMERMAN

• Transmitter often uses conventional or modified antennas placed inside a waveguide section sized to cutoff TEM propagation at the operating frequency (e.g., 1296 MHz experiments).

• Goal: Allow primarily vector potential / momentum wave components to radiate while suppressing classical transverse EM.

• Drive currents typically in the low-Ampere range for experimental links; power levels modest for claimed km-range performance.

Reference: Zimmerman QEX 2011 experiments at McMaster University test range.

HIVELY (EED / SLW)

• Novel antenna designs based on irrotational (gradient-driven) current distributions as disclosed in US Patent 9,306,527.

• These structures are intended to efficiently launch scalar + longitudinal E components (SLW) that may not be produced efficiently by standard Hertzian dipoles.

• Theoretical predictions include SLW with potentially favorable propagation (e.g., different distance scaling or media interaction) compared to classical waves.

Reference: Hively US 9,306,527 B1 (2016); associated EED theory papers.

SLIDE 05

Propagation Characteristics & Range

NIKOLOVA–ZIMMERMAN

Experimental demonstrations at 1.296 GHz with claimed reliable communication over ~1.5 km using vector potential wave components.

~1.5 km demonstrated
UHF (1296 MHz)

Propagation modeled or interpreted in context of potential waves; some tests explored whether effects persist when classical TEM is suppressed (waveguide). Attenuation characteristics subject to ongoing interpretation vs. conventional models.

HIVELY / EED

Theoretical framework predicts SLW with potentially slower attenuation (e.g., closer to 1/r in amplitude in certain validations) and unique interactions with media, including possible reduced skin-effect limitations in conductive environments.

Theoretical long-range potential
Isotropic patterns (monopole)

EED validation criteria (per Reed et al.) include 1/r attenuation evidence, power comparable to TEM, and irrotational current sourcing. Simulations often explore ranges from tabletop to 100+ km and beyond.

Note: Actual propagation depends heavily on frequency, antenna design, media (air, ground, seawater analogs in sims), and exact SLW coupling efficiency. The two approaches may describe overlapping or complementary physical effects.
SLIDE 06

Reception & Detection

NIKOLOVA–ZIMMERMAN APPROACH
Primary Detector: Plasma or fluorescent tubes (often U-shaped or folded) placed in or near waveguide sections or jars.
Mechanism: Tubes respond to time-varying vector potential or associated fields/potentials, producing visible glow or electrical signal differences (including bias-reversal effects used as diagnostic).
Key diagnostic in 2007–2011 McMaster work: signal changes upon DC bias reversal on plasma tubes, interpreted as evidence for vector potential wave detection rather than purely classical E-field.
HIVELY / EED APPROACH
Primary Concept: Detectors sensitive to longitudinal electric field and/or scalar potential gradients. May use structures that couple to irrotational current or field components.
Output: Induced detector current arising from gradient-driven effects in the receiving element, distinct from conventional transverse EM induction.
Per EED and patent: novel receiver geometries can be designed to match the SLW polarization and field structure for efficient conversion of longitudinal E / scalar components into measurable current.
